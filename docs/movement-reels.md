# Movement reels and reusable avatars

The creation launcher and studio distinguish four outputs:

- **Movement reel** generates real articulated character actions. Original reference
  films use their actual source footage; studio designs create a three-scene,
  30-second film in the selected visual world.
- **Avatar reel** uses a fixed-pose portrait with background, camera, typography
  and effect animation. It does not claim to move the character's limbs.
- **Avatar / photo** generates only reusable portrait artwork.
- **Digital card** exports a still invitation with optional saved artwork.

Selecting or reviewing a studio movement reel never silently converts it to
avatar animation. A movement reel is ready only when its owned completed video
matches the chosen template and people. Existing original-film edits retain
their source-video contract and full duration.

Each of the 140 studio designs defaults to a different category-appropriate
signature action. Entrance, signature and finale use three distinct action plans.
The video director treats the saved cast as identity and wardrobe reference,
re-posing its people for scene perspective, joint movement, grounding, lighting
and prop interaction. Character-free studio scenery supplies the environment.
AI likeness and exact choreography remain generative and require preview review.

Reference editing submits at most two independent source parts at a time, after
the reusable cast is ready. Parts are stored by source index and assembled in
source order. A failed part stops new submissions; already submitted neighbours
are saved before the parent reports failure, so a retry can reuse completed work.
Completed dependencies advance in the same polling round. Progress counts saved
steps and elapsed time; it does not invent a completion percentage or ETA.

Saved cast reuse creates no new portrait charge. Written details and unchanged
finished films can be reused even after director prompt improvements. New actions,
people, style or explicit regeneration require a reviewed quote. Provider queue
and rendering latency remain outside the application's control.
